Vision and Objectives

The programme's vision was to build an inclusive, digitally capable research and education community across Asia. That meant closing persistent gaps in connectivity, skills, and access to digital services, especially for Least Developed Countries (LDCs) and Lower-Middle Income Countries (LMICs).

01

Operate a world-class transcontinental network for research and education that reliably links Asia with Europe.

02

Capacity Building

Strengthen human and institutional capacity, with priority support to developing NRENs that need skills, tools, and operating practices to thrive.

03

Public Benefit Services

Support applications and services with public benefit, aligned with national needs and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

These objectives were pursued through regional cooperation, shared standards, and a community-driven model that encouraged local leadership and South–South collaboration.

Stories Behind the Numbers

🏔️

Bhutan - Secure Access from Remote Colleges

With eduroam and national identity federation deployed, students across remote campuses gained secure access to global research and learning platforms. These foundations enabled Bhutan's active role in multi-country virtual classrooms with Bangladesh and Nepal, proving that students in the mountains can participate in the same online seminars as those in capital cities.

👩‍💻

Bangladesh - Women in Cybersecurity

The TechGirl programme trained young women in cybersecurity and advanced networking. Graduates have since co-delivered peer trainings in neighbouring countries, growing a regional community of women digital leaders and expanding the talent pipeline for NRENs and universities.

🏥

Pakistan - Telemedicine for Remote Clinics

A WP5-funded platform linked rural clinics with specialists in urban centres via a central hub. Patients in underserved areas received faster diagnoses and follow-up care, and the model helped spark wider policy conversations about digital health equity.

☁️

Vietnam - Research Data Management

A cloud-based research data platform across VinaREN institutions gave researchers structured access to shared repositories and collaboration tools such as eduroam and federated identity. It moved institutions toward open-science readiness and better stewardship of research outputs.

🌍

LandSAGE - Environmental Resilience

Initially developed for disaster risk in Southeast Asia, LandSAGE added modules for urban flooding, haze tracking, and pandemic mapping. Through cross-border exchanges, teams localised the platform for Nepal and Mongolia, demonstrating how a regional tool can be adapted for diverse ecosystems.

📡

Nepal - Network Transformation

Nepal's link capacity grew from just 45 Mbps to 1,000 Mbps (1 Gbps) - a 22× increase that enabled researchers and students to access global resources at speeds never before possible. This transformation brought Kathmandu into the global research ecosystem.

Project Background

Asi@Connect represents the culmination of decades of Asia-Europe collaboration in research and education networking. Building on the legacy of previous TEIN phases, this initiative brought together stakeholders from across two continents to create lasting digital infrastructure.

🎯

Origins and Purpose

Launched in 2016 as the fifth phase of the Trans-Eurasia Information Network (TEIN) initiative, Asi@Connect built upon two decades of successful Asia-Europe cooperation. The programme addressed persistent digital divides by establishing high-capacity networks connecting 24 countries, with special focus on supporting Least Developed Countries and Lower-Middle Income Countries through targeted capacity building and infrastructure investments.

🏛️

Governance and Consortium Model

The project operated under a sophisticated governance structure led by TEIN*CC as coordinator, with DANTE managing technical operations. A Steering Committee comprising EU and Asian representatives provided strategic oversight, while 24 National Research and Education Networks (NRENs) formed the implementation backbone. This multi-stakeholder model ensured regional ownership and sustainability while maintaining coordination with European networks through GÉANT.

📦

Work Packages and Activities

Implementation was organized into six complementary Work Packages: WP1 focused on network procurement and management; WP2 on capacity development for emerging NRENs; WP3 on advanced services deployment; WP4 on applications and community building; WP5 on innovation for societal benefit; and WP6 on bridging the digital divide. This structure allowed specialized teams to deliver coordinated results while maintaining flexibility to respond to local needs.

💰

Sub-Grant Mechanism and Call Structure

A competitive sub-granting mechanism channeled €11.2 million to 95 projects across the region through six open calls. Proposals underwent rigorous evaluation considering technical merit, regional impact, sustainability, and alignment with programme objectives. This bottom-up innovation approach empowered local institutions to address context-specific challenges while fostering cross-border collaboration and knowledge exchange among research and education communities.

Project Impact

Over nine years, Asi@Connect delivered transformative results across technical infrastructure, human capacity, gender equality, innovative applications, and sustainable development. The programme's impact extended far beyond connectivity metrics to fundamentally reshape how Asian research and education institutions collaborate regionally and globally.

📡

Improved Connectivity and NREN Growth in LDCs

Network capacity exploded from 107 Gbps to 926 Gbps—an 8.7× increase that fundamentally changed what was possible. Nepal's connection jumped 22× from 45 Mbps to 1 Gbps. Bangladesh grew 50× to 5 Gbps. The Asia-Pacific-Europe Ring established redundant 100G links, while 24 countries gained reliable connectivity to global research networks. Emerging NRENs in Bhutan, Laos, and Myanmar matured into sustainable organizations serving their national research communities.

🎓

Capacity Building Outcomes

Over 26,000 individuals across 17,000+ institutions received training through 412 workshops, seminars, and technical exchanges. The TechGirl programme specifically addressed gender gaps by training young women in network engineering and cybersecurity. Train-the-trainer approaches multiplied impact, with graduates conducting peer training in neighboring countries. Regular Network Operations Centre workshops, engineering boot camps, and leadership development programs built sustainable expertise that outlasted project funding.

⚖️

Promoting Gender Equality and Inclusion

Beyond the numbers, Asi@Connect championed inclusion as a core principle. The TechGirl programme trained women from underrepresented communities, many becoming trainers themselves. Project calls explicitly encouraged proposals addressing gender equality concerns. Women's participation in ICT sectors increased measurably in participating countries, while awareness grew among NREN leaders about the importance of diverse technical teams for innovation and sustainability.

💡

Innovative Applications in Health, Education, and Environment

Sub-granted projects delivered tangible societal benefits. Telemedicine networks connected rural clinics with specialist centers, improving healthcare access for underserved populations. E-learning platforms enabled remote education during pandemic lockdowns, while digital libraries provided research access to isolated campuses. Environmental monitoring systems tracked air quality, deforestation, and disaster risks, feeding critical data to policymakers. These applications demonstrated the network's value beyond academic exchange.

🌏

Subgrant Themes and Regional Reach

The 95 sub-granted projects spanned diverse themes: 24 focused on network infrastructure and services; 23 on e-learning and digital education; 18 on e-health and telemedicine; 15 on environmental monitoring and climate action; 10 on cultural heritage preservation; and 5 on agricultural technology. Projects achieved significant regional distribution, with emphasis on LDCs. South-South collaboration flourished as institutions in Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Bhutan partnered on shared challenges.

🎯

Alignment with SDGs

Every activity contributed to multiple UN Sustainable Development Goals. The programme directly advanced SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) through network deployment; SDG 4 (Quality Education) via e-learning platforms; SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being) through telemedicine; SDG 5 (Gender Equality) via targeted training; SDG 13 (Climate Action) through environmental monitoring; SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) by connecting underserved regions; and SDG 17 (Partnerships) through fostering international research collaboration.

Detailed Work Package Structure

WP1 - Network Procurement & Management

+

Focused on establishing and maintaining core backbone connectivity. Absorbed ~45% of the project budget, reflecting the priority of continuously enhancing the e-infrastructure. TEIN*CC managed WP1 centrally, negotiating capacity contracts that dramatically lowered unit bandwidth costs for partners.

  • Upgraded backbone links to 10–100 Gbps
  • Added 100G Asia-Europe links (Singapore – London, Singapore – Hong Kong)
  • Established Asia-Pacific Europe "AER" Ring for redundancy
  • Network capacity grew from 107 Gbps to 926 Gbps (8.7× growth)

WP2 - Capacity Development of Developing NRENs

+

Strengthened human and organizational capacity of NRENs in Least Developed and Lower-Middle Income countries through training programs, workshops, and staff exchanges.

  • Train-the-trainer approaches to multiply impact locally
  • Foundation courses for engineers from Bhutan, Nepal, Laos
  • Annual NREN NOC (Network Operations Centre) trainings
  • NREN business models and stakeholder engagement training

WP3 - Network Design & Advanced Services

+

Designed new R&E network architectures and introduced advanced services alongside higher-level technical training.

  • Deployed eduroam roaming Wi-Fi and identity federations
  • Established distributed AI/Big Data testbed across multiple countries
  • Provided hands-on training in network security and cloud services
  • Science DMZ for big data optimization

WP4 - Applications, Services & Community Building

+

Deployed specialized network applications and fostered user communities around collaboration tools and platforms.

  • Rollout of eduroam to Myanmar, Vietnam and other countries
  • Identity federation services for single sign-on
  • Teleconferencing systems and digital libraries
  • Regional working groups in security and cloud computing

WP5 - Innovation for Societal Benefit

+

Promoted research and education collaborations that directly benefit society, especially in health, agriculture, environment, and culture.

  • Telemedicine networks for remote diagnostics
  • Environmental monitoring systems and air quality sensors
  • Smart city air monitoring for public health
  • Disaster management using space data applications

WP6 - Bridging the Digital Divide

+

Extended internet access to underserved rural and remote areas in developing countries, reducing connectivity inequities.

  • Community-managed networks in Pakistan's Gilgit-Baltistan
  • Connected villages in Bhutan to campuses in Laos
  • Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) in Nepal and Myanmar
  • Local caching solutions for improved sustainability

Resilience Through Disruption

The programme spanned years of rapid change and significant shocks. Partners dealt with pandemics, natural disasters, and periods of political uncertainty. Even so, services grew and collaborations deepened. Several factors explain this resilience:

🔄 Regional Redundancy

Shared operations reduced service disruption and allowed traffic to be rerouted when needed.

🌐 Communities of Practice

Established networks kept training and collaboration alive online when travel stopped.

🔧 Open Standards

Common tools meant solutions built in one country could be reused in another with minimal friction.

🏠 Local Ownership

When projects proved helpful, teams found ways to keep them going, even in lean times.

"We used to wait weeks for access to essential research articles. Today, with just a click, we connect with scientists across Asia and Europe in real time." — Project Beneficiary, Kathmandu University, Nepal

Conclusion and Outlook

As Asi@Connect concludes, its legacy extends far beyond the immediate technical achievements. The programme has fundamentally reshaped how research and education institutions across Asia connect, collaborate, and contribute to global knowledge creation. Looking forward, the foundations laid here will support continued growth and innovation.

🌱

Sustainability and Regional Equity

The programme's greatest achievement may be its sustainability model. By prioritizing local ownership, capacity building, and institutional strengthening, Asi@Connect ensured that benefits outlast project funding. NRENs that were nascent organizations in 2016 now operate as mature, self-sustaining entities serving their national research communities. The sub-granted projects created lasting infrastructure and trained personnel who continue advancing their missions. Regional cooperation mechanisms—peer networks, knowledge-sharing platforms, and collaborative governance structures—remain active, enabling continued South-South exchange. Most importantly, the programme demonstrated that bridging digital divides requires more than infrastructure; it demands sustained investment in people, institutions, and partnerships that empower communities to chart their own digital futures.

🤝

Ongoing Partnerships and Next Steps

While Asi@Connect formally concludes, the partnerships and momentum it generated continue. Many participating NRENs have secured national government support to maintain and expand their networks. Regional working groups in cybersecurity, federated identity, and research data management persist as active communities. The European Commission and Asian governments are exploring successor programmes building on proven models. Key priorities for the future include: expanding coverage to remaining unconnected institutions and underserved regions; enhancing cybersecurity capabilities as threats evolve; supporting emerging technologies like AI, IoT, and edge computing; strengthening research data infrastructure for open science; and deepening integration with global research networks. The relationships, trust, and shared practices developed through Asi@Connect provide a strong foundation for continued Asia-Europe collaboration in research and education networking.

Glossary of Key Terms

NREN

National Research and Education Network - A specialized network serving the research, education, and innovation community of a country

TEIN

Trans-Eurasia Information Network - The Asia-Europe research network connecting participating countries

eduroam

education roaming - Secure Wi-Fi roaming service allowing researchers and students to access internet at participating institutions worldwide

LDC

Least Developed Country - UN classification for countries facing severe structural impediments to sustainable development

LMIC

Lower-Middle Income Country - World Bank classification for countries with specific GNI per capita ranges

SDG

Sustainable Development Goals - UN's 17 global goals for achieving sustainable development by 2030

Gbps

Gigabits per second - Unit measuring network data transfer speed (1 Gbps = 1,000 Mbps)

Identity Federation

System allowing users to access multiple services with single institutional credentials through trusted authentication

NOC

Network Operations Centre - Centralized facility monitoring, managing, and maintaining network infrastructure

Peering

Direct interconnection between networks enabling efficient data exchange without intermediary transit providers

Science DMZ

Network architecture designed to handle high-volume scientific data transfers with minimal security-related performance impacts

Work Package (WP)

Organized grouping of project activities with defined objectives, deliverables, and responsible partners
